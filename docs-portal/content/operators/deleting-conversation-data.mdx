# Deleting conversation data

When someone asks you to delete what your agent holds about them, delete their conversation, or delete the visitor and every conversation they had. Either way the deletion is permanent: Radioso removes the messages and everything it derived from them in one step, and there is no undo.

## Delete one conversation

In the dashboard, open the conversation from the Inbox, choose **Open in debug view**, then **Delete conversation**. Radioso asks you to confirm, because the deletion is permanent. The conversation leaves every teammate's Inbox lists, and a drawer someone else had open on it closes.

Over the API, send:

```http
DELETE /api/v1/history/chat/{conversationId}
```

A deleted conversation answers `204` with no body. A conversation the workspace does not have answers `404`, and nothing is recorded.

## Delete a visitor

A visitor groups the conversations one browser, or one verified customer, had with your agents. To delete the person rather than a single conversation, take the `visitor.id` from the conversation detail (`GET /api/v1/history/chat/{conversationId}`) and send:

```http
DELETE /api/v1/history/visitors/{visitorId}
```

Radioso deletes each of the visitor's conversations as above, then the customer-scoped context values your site pushed for the visitor's verified customer id, then the voice calls the website widget started for the visitor that never reached a first turn, recordings included, then the visitor record itself. Visitor deletion is available over the API; the dashboard deletes one conversation at a time.

## Delete one person across channels

A person who also emailed you, or asked your agent in Slack, has conversations no visitor record groups. [Answering a data subject request](/operators/data-subject-requests) finds them by verified customer id, email address or Slack member id, exports what Radioso holds about them, and erases the visitors and conversations of the identities you confirm in one request.

## Who can delete

Both routes need `workspace.history.delete`. Owners and admins of the workspace hold it — an organization member granted admin on the workspace counts as an admin there; members can read history but cannot delete it. An admin personal credential or an admin service-account credential can call both routes, so a data-subject request pipeline can delete over the API without a signed-in session.

Each deletion records one `conversation.erased` or `visitor.erased` audit event: who asked, the conversation or visitor id, and how many conversations, messages, and other rows went. It never records any of the content. That event is your record that the request was honoured.

## What is deleted

Deleting a conversation removes:

- its messages, summary and title, and the version of the privacy notice its visitor was shown;
- routine state, including the values a routine collected, pending approvals, clarifications, and directive memory;
- held replies, ownership, activity, and delivery failures;
- answer feedback and triage, coverage assessments, facets and topic memberships;
- eval snapshots taken from it, with the eval cases and runs built on them;
- email threads, sent-email records, and the inbound mail it received, raw message included;
- Slack and voice links, and WhatsApp contacts;
- a voice call's recording, which ElevenLabs keeps: Radioso asks ElevenLabs to delete it once the conversation is gone (see below);
- the WhatsApp message log of the number whose latest conversation it is (see below);
- routine actions and notices queued for it and not yet sent;
- email skill activity, including its recipient summary;
- session-scoped context values for a chat session no other conversation uses;
- the visitor record, when this was an anonymous visitor's last conversation;
- the turn audit events that carry its content: suggestions, rewritten queries, retrieval diagnostics, and traces.

## What is kept

A few records stay on purpose. None of them holds the conversation's content:

- **A verified visitor's record.** It belongs to the customer, not to one conversation, so deleting their last conversation leaves it in place with a conversation count of zero. It stays the handle for a later [visitor deletion](#delete-a-visitor), which is what removes their customer-scoped context values.
- **The billing ledger.** Usage events keep their token counts and lose the link to the conversation and its messages.
- **The notice email cap.** The daily count of notice emails keeps its numbers, so deleting a conversation never hands its emails back to the day's budget.
- **The Enterprise Edition reply quota.** The per-period counter keeps the conversation id and a count, so deleting conversations cannot reset a plan's quota.
- **Operational audit events.** Events that name the conversation id with an outcome — a takeover, an approval, an email send — keep the id and the outcome codes.
- **Audience Pulse topics.** Topic names and descriptions are written from many visitors' de-identified question summaries and checked for personal details, and topic sizes are counts. They stay. The Audience Pulse report itself quotes visitors' questions in its recommendations, so a report built on a deleted conversation is discarded; regenerate it from what remains.

WhatsApp keeps one transport log per phone number, not per conversation, and links the number only to its latest conversation; a message after the conversation timeout starts a new conversation. Deleting a number's latest conversation therefore deletes the number's whole log, earlier conversations' entries included. Deleting one of its earlier conversations leaves the log until the latest one is deleted.

Two copies outside the conversation stay until their own lifecycle removes them:

- A private test started with **Continue in test chat** holds its own copy of the thread. It goes when the agent or the workspace is deleted.
- A Ray conversation that read the transcript keeps what Ray wrote about it until Ray's 90-day retention removes it.

## What deletion cannot recall

Deletion reaches what Radioso stores. It cannot reach what already left:

- operator notices already sent by email or posted to Slack;
- webhook payloads already delivered to another system;
- emails your agent already sent to the visitor;
- anything a model provider received while answering, under that provider's own terms.

Database backups and logs keep deleted data until they age out: on Radioso Cloud, seven daily backups, seven days of point-in-time recovery logs where recovery is on, and 30 days of logs. See [Data retention](/operators/data-retention).

A voice call's recording lives in your ElevenLabs account, so the deletion writes down each recording to delete, with an encrypted copy of the workspace's ElevenLabs key, in the same database transaction as the deletion itself. A background job then asks ElevenLabs to delete each one, usually within seconds, four at a time, and the deletion itself never waits on ElevenLabs. A recording ElevenLabs no longer holds counts as deleted. Each recording ElevenLabs refuses, or does not answer for, gets its own retries: up to eight asks, waiting a minute, then five, fifteen, an hour, three, six and twelve hours between them, about a day in all. When ElevenLabs answers that it is getting too many requests, the job stops asking for as long as ElevenLabs says. A worker that restarts in between picks up where the last one stopped, and the copy of the key goes as soon as no recording waiting to be deleted needs it.

Each recording deleted is recorded as a `conversation.recording_deleted` audit event with `outcome` `deleted`, or `already_absent` when ElevenLabs no longer held it.

### Recordings left in ElevenLabs

A recording still there after its eighth ask stays in your ElevenLabs account. So does every recording of a workspace that held no ElevenLabs key when it was deleted, and those are given up on at once. To see them, an owner or admin of the organization calls `GET /api/v1/account/voice-recordings/left-at-vendor` with their dashboard session. It lists every recording your organization's deletions left behind, deleted workspaces included, newest first. Each entry has the ElevenLabs conversation id (`vendorConversationId`), its region, the conversation and workspace it came from, why it was left (`reason`, such as `vendor_rejected` or `credential_missing`) and when (`failedAt`). Delete each one in ElevenLabs by that id. Radioso also records each as a `conversation.recording_deleted` event with status `failure`, and reports it to the deployment's error tracking.

Once the organization itself is deleted, nobody in it can call that endpoint. Only the deployment's operators see what its deletion left at ElevenLabs, in their error tracking and in the database ([Self-hosting operations](/operators/self-hosting-operations)). Once the workspace's ElevenLabs key is removed, or replaced with one for another ElevenLabs account, Radioso cannot delete the old account's recordings at all.

## Deleting everything at once

Deleting a workspace deletes every conversation and visitor in it, with the same cleanup, voice call recordings included: the list of recordings to delete and the key copy outlive the workspace, and the organization, until the job is done. It also deletes the files the workspace stored: the original of every uploaded document and every agent logo. Deleting the organization (**Settings → Workspace → Delete organization**, owners only) deletes every workspace it has, files included.

The files go once the deletion has gone through. The deletion hands them to the worker in the same database transaction, so the workspace and the hand-off commit together or not at all, and the worker deletes the files in batches as soon as it picks the job up. A delete that storage refuses is tried again, with growing waits, for about a day; a file that is already gone counts as deleted. Each run of that job records one `workspace.stored_files.deleted` audit event with the deleted workspace's id and how many files went, never their names; a workspace with more than 1,000 files takes more than one run.

## Common failure modes

- **`403`** — the caller lacks `workspace.history.delete`: a member session, or a member credential.
- **`404`** — the conversation or visitor belongs to another workspace, or is already gone.
- **`500` on a conversation, visitor or workspace with voice calls** — Radioso could not read where the calls' recordings are, or the workspace's ElevenLabs key, so it deleted nothing rather than lose them. Send the same request again.
- **A recording in `GET /api/v1/account/voice-recordings/left-at-vendor`** — ElevenLabs kept refusing for about a day, or Radioso had no key to ask with. Delete it in ElevenLabs by its `vendorConversationId`.
- **The Delete conversation button is missing** — your role on this workspace is member: neither your organization role nor a workspace grant makes you an admin here. Ask an owner or admin, or ask for an admin grant on the workspace.
