# Email channel

The email channel lets a customer write to an address they already know — `support@customer.com` — and have a Radioso agent sit behind it, without Radioso taking over that mailbox. You forward your existing address to a relay address Radioso gives you, and once you've added a couple of DNS records, replies go out as your real address again.

Every email the channel accepts is visible to an operator somewhere: as a conversation in the Inbox, or as an event in the mailbox's event log when it never becomes one. "The agent doesn't act" never means "nobody can see it."

You manage it from an agent's **Channels**, alongside Web chat, API, MCP, and Slack.

## What it does

- Each mailbox you add gets its own opaque relay address on an inbound domain Radioso operates. Forwarding your real address to it is the only setup receiving needs — no DNS at all.
- A new mailbox runs in `draft` mode: the agent writes a reply to every accepted email, and it waits in the Inbox for you to send, edit, or discard — nothing goes to the customer without that review. Switch a mailbox to `operator_only` and the agent is out of the loop entirely: every accepted email opens or continues a human-owned conversation, and no turn ever runs on it.
- Email conversations show up in the Inbox exactly like any other channel, and they never auto-close on their own. What decides whether one needs attention is whether a person has to act on it — not how long it's been quiet.
- Replies go out as your real address — `support@customer.com`, not the relay — once you've verified sending for that domain.
- An email that doesn't turn into a conversation (an autoresponder, a bounce, spam, a message to a mailbox you've disabled) is still recorded: you can see it in the mailbox's event log with its sender, subject, and why it stopped there.
- Everything the agent does on its own has a limit — an hourly draft budget, a per-thread send budget, one draft per burst of emails — and hitting one hands the email to a person. See [Limits](#limits).

## Connect a mailbox

Add a mailbox and bind it to an agent. Radioso returns a relay address on its inbound domain — forward your real address to it from whatever mail service you already use, and your mailbox stays exactly where it is. Run the setup check and send a message to your real address; once it arrives through the relay, the mailbox shows `receiving: ok` with the time it landed.

**Google Workspace and Gmail** confirm a new forwarding address before they'll use it: they email a confirmation code or link to the destination, which here is the relay address. That confirmation email shows up as an event on your mailbox — open its raw view to read the code and enter it on the Google side.

**Microsoft 365** blocks external automatic forwarding until a tenant admin changes the outbound anti-spam policy to allow it. Do that first; only then create the forwarding rule pointed at the relay address, or the forward fails with a bounce instead of going through.

A mailbox that's received mail before and then gone quiet past its silence threshold shows a notice with the last time anything arrived — a dropped forwarding rule is invisible to Radioso otherwise, so this is the only signal you get.

## Verify sending

Adding a mailbox registers its domain for sending if it isn't registered already. The settings card shows the DKIM and SPF records (and a DMARC recommendation) with a copy button and a status per record. They're additive — adding them doesn't touch anything else already set up on your domain.

Until a domain verifies, the mailbox still receives mail normally, but a reply is refused before anything is written, with the missing step named. Retrying once the domain verifies is safe. A domain can only belong to one workspace: adding one already claimed elsewhere is refused without saying who has it.

## Reply and track delivery

Replying to an email conversation works like replying on any other channel — it goes out as your mailbox's real address, in the same thread the customer wrote to, once that domain's sending records have verified.

Each outbound message shows a delivery state: `queued`, `accepted`, `delivered`, `bounced`, `failed`, `uncertain`, or `halted`. The last four flag the conversation for attention, with the reason shown on the message.

A send that comes back `uncertain` means its outcome never resolved with the provider. Radioso retries it safely on its own inside the provider's idempotency window — nothing you need to do. Only once that window closes unresolved do you get a decision to make: mark it sent, once you've confirmed delivery some other way, or ask for a resend, which is recorded against you. Radioso never resends a send by itself.

## Review a draft

On a `draft` mailbox, an accepted email doesn't go straight to the agent's answer box — it gets a review turn, and the reply comes back to you first. The agent can't run an action or start a routine while it's drafting, and nothing it writes reaches the customer until you act. In the Inbox, the draft shows the turn's outcome (Answered, No matching documents, Out of scope, Answer unavailable) and, if it needed an action that didn't run, a badge naming which one — the draft text itself never claims that action happened.

You have three moves on a draft:

- **Send it.** It goes out as written, attributed to the agent.
- **Edit it, then send.** It goes out with your wording, attributed to you — the original draft is kept, so anyone who checks later can see what the agent actually wrote.
- **Discard it.** Nothing goes out. The conversation stays flagged until someone replies or takes it over, and the next email on the thread gets a fresh draft.

Sending or discarding never takes the conversation over — it stays with the agent, same as before the draft was written. Only a free-form reply or an explicit takeover hands it to a person. If a newer email arrives, a teammate replies, or someone takes the conversation over while a draft is still waiting, that draft is dropped in favor of the newer thing — you'll never see two drafts competing for the same reply.

## Limits

One autoresponder or one burst of new threads could run up model spend in minutes if nothing capped it. Each limit below hands the work to a person when it's hit, so nothing accepted is lost. Set them per mailbox through the mailbox API.

| Mailbox setting | What it caps | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `hourlyGenerationBudget` | Drafts the mailbox writes per hour | 30 |
| `threadSendBudget` | The agent's automatic sends on one thread before you renew it | 3 |
| `threadContextMessages` | How many of the thread's newest messages a draft reads | 10 |

**When the hourly budget runs out**, mail keeps arriving but stops getting drafts: each email opens or continues a conversation handed to a person with the reason `generation_budget`, in the Inbox like any other handoff. The hour is a fixed window that starts with the first draft after the previous window closed, and drafts resume when it rolls over. A conversation already handed to a person stays with them until you hand it back.

**The thread send budget** counts the agent's automatic sends on a thread. Your reply on the thread renews it, and so does sending a draft, as written or edited. Customer mail never renews it. When it's full, the next reply waits for you with the reason `send_budget`. On a `draft` mailbox every reply goes through you, so the count stays at zero.

**Bursts share one draft.** Emails on one thread inside the deployment's coalescing window (`EMAIL_CHANNEL_COALESCE_SECONDS`, 60 seconds by default) get a single draft answering all of them.

**Automated mail never gets a reply.** Radioso reads the headers — never the subject or body — and keeps the agent away from any email with:

- `Auto-Submitted` set to anything other than `no`
- `Precedence: bulk`, `list`, or `junk`
- `X-Auto-Response-Suppress`
- `List-Id`
- a delivery status report, where a bounce of something Radioso sent marks that reply `bounced`
- a `From` that's one of your own mailboxes

Each one shows in the mailbox's event log, and as a note on the conversation when it lands on a thread that already has one. A responder that sends none of these headers looks like a person; the budgets catch it instead.

**Mail keeps the mode it arrived under, or a more cautious one.** An email runs under whichever gives the agent less autonomy: the mailbox's mode when the email arrived, or its mode now. Switch a mailbox from `draft` to `operator_only` and mail still waiting for a draft goes to a person; switch it back and only mail that arrives afterwards gets drafts.

## Direct receiving (advanced)

Instead of forwarding, you can point an entire customer-owned domain or subdomain's MX record at Radioso. This is a deliberate trade-off: once you confirm it by typing the domain's name, *every* address on that domain routes to Radioso, not just the mailboxes you've connected. Forwarding is the default for a reason — reach for direct receiving only when you want Radioso to be the domain's mail server outright.

## Common failure modes

- **A mailbox never shows `receiving: ok`.** The forwarding rule either isn't saved on the mail-service side, or (Microsoft 365) the tenant's outbound anti-spam policy still blocks external forwarding. Check the policy before the rule.
- **Google's confirmation email never arrives in your own inbox.** It isn't supposed to — it goes to the relay address, because that's the forwarding destination Google is confirming. Read it from the mailbox's event log in Radioso instead.
- **A reply is refused with a missing-step message.** The mailbox's sending domain hasn't verified yet. Add the DKIM and SPF records shown on the domain and wait for them to resolve.
- **Adding a domain is refused without an explanation of who has it.** Another workspace already claimed that domain for sending. Use a different domain or subdomain.
- **An email you expected to see never shows up anywhere.** Check the mailbox's event log — automated-sender mail, bounces, spam, and mail from your own mailboxes are recorded there but never open a conversation, by design.
- **Conversations arrive already handed to a person, with the reason `generation_budget`.** The mailbox wrote its hourly budget of drafts. Drafts resume when the hour rolls over; hand a conversation back to the agent and its next email gets one. If real traffic rather than a flood keeps hitting the budget, raise `hourlyGenerationBudget`.
- **An email from a real customer never got a draft.** Check its disposition in the event log. `automated_sender` means the sender's mail service added an automation header such as `Precedence: bulk` or `List-Id`, often because the message passed through a mailing list. Reply to the customer yourself.
- **A reply shows `bounced` or `failed`.** The customer's address rejected it or the provider couldn't deliver it — the reason is on the message. Acknowledge the flag once you've dealt with it, such as following up another way.
- **A reply shows `halted`.** The mailbox's domain lost its sending verification after the reply was queued, so nothing went out. Re-verify the domain, then resend from the conversation's delivery failure.
- **A reply has been `uncertain` for a while.** Its outcome never came back from the provider. Mark it sent if you've confirmed the customer got it some other way, or ask for a resend — both are recorded against you.
- **Sending a draft says "This mailbox cannot send yet."** The mailbox's sending domain isn't verified. See Verify sending, above — nothing was lost, the draft is still there once it is.
- **Sending a draft says the conversation changed hands, or the mailbox settings changed.** Someone took the conversation over, or the mailbox's mode or agent changed, after the draft was written. Nothing was sent; re-open the conversation to see what's current.
